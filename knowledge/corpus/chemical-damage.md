## Symptoms

Sudden, irregular brown or black patches, spotting, or leaf distortion appear
after exposure to a cleaning product, leaf-shine spray, pesticide overspray, air
freshener, or paint fumes. The pattern is often asymmetric or follows a spray or
contact shape rather than spreading evenly.

## Where on the plant symptoms appear

Wherever contact or exposure occurred — often one side, a few adjacent leaves, or
a random scattering — with no consistent age pattern the way nutrient shortages
have.

## Look-alikes and how to tell them apart

Fertiliser burn also causes sudden damage, but confined to leaf tips and margins
after feeding, sometimes with a salt crust on the soil, rather than a random
contact pattern. Sunscald patches sit only on the side of the leaf facing strong
light and follow a light change, not a chemical exposure. Frost damage blackens
tissue after cold exposure with an initial water-soaked look, unlike the dry,
irregular patches chemical contact leaves.

## Confirming test the user can perform

Think back over the last few days for any sprays, cleaning, polishing, or fumes
used near the plant. Damage that is patchy, asymmetric, and traceable to a
specific product or event, rather than following a leaf-age or light-position
pattern, points to chemical contact.

## Treatment, least-invasive first

Move the plant away from the source and ventilate the room. Rinse leaves gently
with plain water to remove any residue still on the surface. Trim off leaves that
are severely damaged, since the tissue will not recover. Avoid leaf-shine
products and keep sprays of any kind away from foliage in future.

## Expected time to visible improvement

New growth should emerge unaffected within two to three weeks once exposure
stops. Existing damaged tissue does not recover.

## Prognosis and when to give up

Good, provided exposure was a one-off event and growth points were not directly
soaked. Repeated exposure to the same product will cause cumulative damage.
