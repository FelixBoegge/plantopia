## Symptoms

A leaf or stem shows a clean tear, crease, snapped point, or bruised patch that
matches a specific event such as a knock, a fall, rough handling, or a curious
pet. The damage is confined to the affected spot and does not spread or appear
on new growth.

## Where on the plant symptoms appear

Wherever the impact or contact occurred, with no consistent leaf-age or
leaf-position pattern the way a nutrient or pest problem would show.

## Look-alikes and how to tell them apart

Caterpillar or slug damage leaves chewed or grazed edges with frass or a slime
trail, not the clean tear or crease a mechanical knock produces. Chemical damage
causes irregular brown or black patches from a contact substance rather than a
visible physical tear or snapped stem. Cold draught blackens tissue tied to a
cold air source rather than showing a crease or break consistent with an impact.

## Confirming test the user can perform

Examine the damage for a clean tear, crease, or snapped point that matches a
specific recent event, rather than a chewed edge, dark droppings, slime trail, or
gradual discoloration spreading over several days.

## Treatment, least-invasive first

Trim any hanging or badly torn tissue cleanly with sterilised scissors so it does
not tear further or invite rot at the wound. Leave minor damage alone otherwise,
since it will not worsen. Keep the damaged area dry and avoid further handling of
that spot. No biological or chemical treatment is needed for a mechanical injury.

## Expected time to visible improvement

The damage itself does not heal, but new growth should be unaffected within one
to two weeks.

## Prognosis and when to give up

Excellent in most cases. Only damage to the main growing point or a significant
portion of the main stem sets the plant back meaningfully, and even then it is
rarely fatal.
