## Symptoms

A white, powdery coating appears on leaf surfaces, stems, and sometimes buds,
starting as small circular patches that spread until they resemble a dusting of
talcum powder. Affected leaves can yellow, curl, or become distorted underneath
the coating. It develops readily in still, humid air with moderate temperatures.

## Where on the plant symptoms appear

Any leaf can be affected, but it often starts on lower or more shaded leaves and
in crowded parts of the plant where airflow is poorest, then spreads upward.

## Look-alikes and how to tell them apart

Mealybugs also leave a white residue, but as discrete cottony clumps tucked into
leaf axils rather than an even dusty film across the leaf surface. Sooty mould is
black and sits as a surface film on honeydew rather than a white powder embedded
in the leaf. Salt buildup leaves a white crust on the soil surface, not on the
leaves themselves.

## Confirming test the user can perform

Rub a patch of the white coating gently with a fingertip. Powdery mildew smears
like fine flour and comes off visibly on the finger, unlike a fixed insect or a
natural leaf marking.

## Treatment, least-invasive first

Improve airflow around the plant by spacing it away from other plants and
avoiding overhead misting. Remove and discard the most heavily coated leaves.
Wipe milder patches away with a damp cloth. A weekly spray of a dilute
bicarbonate-of-soda solution can slow spread as a gentle intervention. Only if
it continues to spread, apply a fungicide labelled for powdery mildew, following
the product label exactly.

## Expected time to visible improvement

No new white patches within one to two weeks of improving airflow. Existing
coated leaves do not clear fully but new growth should emerge clean.

## Prognosis and when to give up

Good with airflow correction. Recurrence points to a persistently humid,
still spot that needs a permanent change rather than repeated one-off treatment.
