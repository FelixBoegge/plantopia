## Symptoms

Growth slows and new leaves emerge smaller and paler than older ones. The plant
leans or stretches toward the nearest window or light source. Variegated plants
often revert to solid green as chlorophyll production increases to compensate.
Lower, older leaves may yellow and drop as the plant sheds growth it can no
longer support.

## Where on the plant symptoms appear

New growth is smallest and palest; the whole plant orients toward the light
source over time. Older lower leaves are the ones that yellow and drop first when
the shortage is severe enough to force leaf shedding.

## Look-alikes and how to tell them apart

Etiolation is the extreme version of this same problem: pale, thin, widely spaced
stem growth reaching for light, whereas plain insufficient light more often shows
as smaller, duller leaves and leaning without dramatic stem stretching. Nitrogen
deficiency also yellows and drops old leaves, but growth stays a normal size and
shape rather than leaning or paling overall. Dormancy slows growth seasonally too,
but without leaning, paling, or variegation loss.

## Confirming test the user can perform

Check how far the plant is from its light source and whether it visibly leans or
turns toward a window. Compare a new leaf with an older one: if the new leaf is
noticeably smaller, paler, or less variegated, insufficient light is likely.

## Treatment, least-invasive first

Move the plant closer to a bright window, or rotate it regularly so growth does
not lean permanently to one side. Clean dusty leaves so they can use available
light efficiently. If natural light is genuinely limited, add a grow light on a
timer for supplemental hours. There is no chemical fix for a light shortage.

## Expected time to visible improvement

New growth should return to normal size and colour within four to six weeks of
increasing light. Existing pale or reverted leaves stay as they are.

## Prognosis and when to give up

Excellent once adequate light is provided. A plant that continues to decline
despite bright light likely has a second, unrelated problem.
