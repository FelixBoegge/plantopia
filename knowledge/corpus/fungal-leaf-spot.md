## Symptoms

Roughly circular to oval brown or tan spots appear on leaves, often with a
darker border and sometimes concentric rings giving a target-like appearance.
The centre of an older spot is dry and papery rather than soft or wet, and tiny
dark dots (fruiting bodies) can sometimes be seen within it.

## Where on the plant symptoms appear

Scattered across leaves of any age, though older, lower leaves often show the
most spots since they have been exposed the longest.

## Look-alikes and how to tell them apart

Bacterial leaf spot produces angular, water-soaked, greasy-looking spots bounded
by veins rather than fungal leaf spot's dry, round, target-like pattern.
Anthracnose forms larger, sunken lesions concentrated at leaf tips and margins
rather than scattered round spots across the leaf. Rust produces orange, powdery
pustules mainly on the leaf underside, not dry brown circular spots on top.

## Confirming test the user can perform

Examine a spot with a hand lens for concentric rings or tiny black dots at its
centre, and press it gently: a dry, papery texture rather than a soft, wet feel
supports a fungal rather than bacterial cause.

## Treatment, least-invasive first

Improve airflow and avoid wetting leaves when watering, since fungal spores
spread readily in splashed water and still, humid air. Remove and discard
spotted leaves promptly. Disinfect tools between cuts. Space the plant away from
others while active. If spotting continues to spread, apply a fungicide labelled
for leaf spot, following the product label exactly.

## Expected time to visible improvement

No new spots within one to two weeks of improving airflow and removing affected
leaves.

## Prognosis and when to give up

Good. Fungal leaf spot is rarely fatal on its own; persistent spread despite dry
conditions and leaf removal suggests a chronic environmental cause needs fixing
too.
