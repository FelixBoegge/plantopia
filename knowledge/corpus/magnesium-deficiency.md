## Symptoms

Older leaves develop a blotchy, marbled yellowing between the veins, usually
starting at the leaf margin and spreading inward, while the veins themselves stay
green. In advanced cases the yellowed patches can take on a reddish or purple
tinge. The leaf shape and texture stay normal.

## Where on the plant symptoms appear

The oldest, lowest leaves first, because magnesium is mobile and the plant
relocates it to new growth when supply is short. The newest leaves at the tip
remain fully green.

## Look-alikes and how to tell them apart

Iron deficiency produces the same marbled, interveinal pattern but on the newest
leaves at the growing tip rather than the old ones, since iron cannot be
relocated by the plant. Nitrogen deficiency also fades old leaves but yellows the
whole leaf evenly, veins included, rather than leaving a blotchy pattern with
green veins. Potassium deficiency browns a solid band around the leaf margin
rather than producing scattered interveinal blotches.

## Confirming test the user can perform

Check the two or three oldest leaves for a marbled, blotchy yellow pattern between
veins that remain green. If the newest leaves at the tip are unaffected and fully
green, magnesium deficiency, rather than iron, is the likely cause.

## Treatment, least-invasive first

Refresh old, depleted potting mix at the next repot. A simple cultural fix is to
water occasionally with a dilute Epsom salt solution, which supplies magnesium
directly and is gentle enough for most houseplants when used sparingly. If
marbling continues to spread, apply a fertiliser that includes magnesium,
following the product label's rate and frequency exactly.

## Expected time to visible improvement

New leaves should emerge without marbling within three to four weeks. Existing
yellowed patches on old leaves do not revert to green.

## Prognosis and when to give up

Good. Magnesium deficiency responds reliably to correction; persistent marbling
on new growth despite treatment suggests iron deficiency is also present.
