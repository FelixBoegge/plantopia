## Symptoms

Small orange, yellow, or rust-brown powdery pustules develop, mostly on leaf
undersides, with corresponding pale or yellow spots visible on the upper leaf
surface directly above them. The powdery pustules rub off easily, leaving orange
residue on anything that touches them.

## Where on the plant symptoms appear

Leaf undersides carry the pustules; the upper surface shows only the matching
yellow spot. Older, lower leaves are usually affected first and most heavily.

## Look-alikes and how to tell them apart

Powdery mildew produces a white, dusty coating on the leaf surface generally,
not orange pustules specific to the underside. Fungal leaf spot produces dry
brown circular spots without the loose, powdery orange dust rust leaves behind.
Spider mites cause stippling and webbing rather than powdery pustules that
transfer colour onto a fingertip or tissue.

## Confirming test the user can perform

Rub the underside of an affected leaf gently with a white tissue or paper. An
orange or rust-coloured powder transfers visibly onto it, confirming rust rather
than a leaf spot or pest.

## Treatment, least-invasive first

Remove and discard affected leaves promptly, since pustules release spores that
spread to healthy leaves. Improve airflow and avoid wetting leaves when
watering. Space the plant away from others while active. Clean up any fallen
leaves nearby. Only if spread continues, apply a fungicide labelled for rust,
following the product label exactly.

## Expected time to visible improvement

No new pustules within one to two weeks of removing affected leaves and
improving airflow.

## Prognosis and when to give up

Good if caught early and affected leaves are removed promptly. Widespread rust
covering most of the plant's leaves is a poor sign and may warrant starting a
replacement from healthy cuttings instead.
