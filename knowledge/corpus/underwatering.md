## Symptoms

Leaves wilt, curl inward, and feel thin or papery. Edges and tips turn brown and
crisp. The soil pulls away from the pot sides and feels light. Lower leaves may
yellow and drop, but the plant perks up noticeably within hours of a thorough
watering.

## Where on the plant symptoms appear

Whole plant wilts together rather than starting at the base. Leaf tips and margins
brown first; older leaves are usually the first to yellow and drop if drought is
repeated.

## Look-alikes and how to tell them apart

Root rot also wilts, but the soil is wet and the plant does not recover after
watering; the roots are mushy rather than merely dry. Overwatering yellows leaves
with soft brown patches while the soil stays wet for days. Low humidity browns only
the leaf edges without the whole-plant wilt and limp texture underwatering causes.

## Confirming test the user can perform

Push a finger into the soil to the second knuckle. If it is dry and the pot feels
light when lifted, the plant is underwatered. Water thoroughly and check again in
two hours: real underwatering shows visible recovery by then.

## Treatment, least-invasive first

Water thoroughly until it runs from the drainage holes, then let the top few
centimetres dry before watering again. If the pot feels light and water runs
straight through, the medium may have shrunk away from the roots; stand the pot in
a saucer of water for ten minutes to rehydrate it evenly. Set a watering reminder
matched to the plant's actual drying rate rather than a fixed calendar schedule.

## Expected time to visible improvement

Turgor returns within a few hours of watering. Browned tips and margins do not
heal, but new growth should emerge normal within one to two weeks.

## Prognosis and when to give up

Excellent if caught before leaves fully desiccate. Repeated severe drought
stress will eventually kill roots outright, so consistency matters more than any
single rescue watering.
