## Symptoms

A single older leaf, usually near the base or interior of the plant, gradually
turns an even yellow to brown and drops, while the rest of the plant, including
new growth, stays green and turgid. It happens occasionally as part of normal
leaf turnover rather than as a spreading problem.

## Where on the plant symptoms appear

The oldest leaves, one at a time, rather than a whole band of lower leaves
yellowing together. New growth is completely unaffected.

## Look-alikes and how to tell them apart

Nitrogen deficiency yellows several old leaves together in a broad band while
still allowing new growth, unlike senescence's single-leaf-at-a-time pattern.
Overwatering yellows leaves with soft, wet brown patches on wet soil, whereas a
senescing leaf dries and browns without softening or wet patches. Underwatering
wilts before browning; a senescing leaf browns in place while the rest of the
plant, and even the leaf itself before it drops, stays turgid rather than limp.

## Confirming test the user can perform

Count how many leaves are affected at once and check the rest of the plant. A
single old leaf near the base or interior turning colour and dropping every few
weeks, with new growth and all other leaves staying green and firm, confirms
normal senescence rather than a disorder.

## Treatment, least-invasive first

No treatment is needed. Once a leaf has fully browned, remove it by gently
pulling or snipping it at the base for appearance. Continue normal watering,
light, and feeding as usual.

## Expected time to visible improvement

Immediate — removing the spent leaf tidies the plant right away, and new growth
is unaffected.

## Prognosis and when to give up

Excellent. This is normal, healthy plant behaviour, not a disorder, and requires
no correction.
