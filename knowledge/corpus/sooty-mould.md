## Symptoms

A black, sooty, powdery coating develops on leaf and stem surfaces. It sits on
top of the tissue rather than growing into it, and wipes off fairly easily with
a damp cloth to reveal a normal green leaf underneath. It grows on the sticky
honeydew left behind by sap-sucking pests elsewhere on the plant.

## Where on the plant symptoms appear

Wherever honeydew has dripped, typically on the upper surface of leaves
positioned below an infestation of aphids, mealybugs, scale, or whitefly higher
up the plant.

## Look-alikes and how to tell them apart

Powdery mildew is light grey or white and grows into the leaf surface, unlike
sooty mould's black surface film that wipes away cleanly. Botrytis is a fuzzy
grey-brown growth on dead or damaged tissue, not a black film tied to honeydew
from a pest elsewhere on the plant. Because sooty mould only grows where
honeydew exists, its presence is really a sign to search for the pest causing
it, not to treat the mould itself as the primary problem.

## Confirming test the user can perform

Wipe the black coating with a damp cloth; it comes away easily, revealing a
normal leaf surface underneath. Then check stems, leaf axils, and new growth
higher on the plant for aphids, mealybugs, scale, or whitefly, which are the
actual source.

## Treatment, least-invasive first

Identify and treat the sap-sucking pest producing the honeydew, since the
mould itself is only a symptom of that infestation. Wipe affected leaves clean
with a damp cloth once the pest is under control. Improve airflow to help
leaves dry and discourage regrowth of the mould. There is no separate chemical
treatment for the mould itself; any product used should target the pest.

## Expected time to visible improvement

The mould stops spreading as soon as honeydew production stops; existing
coating can be wiped clean immediately.

## Prognosis and when to give up

Excellent. Sooty mould causes little direct harm beyond blocking light from the
leaf surface, and clears completely once the underlying pest is resolved.
