## Symptoms

New leaves emerge distorted, puckered, or crinkled rather than lying flat, and
their tips or margins may show small brown, dead patches. New stems can be weak or
prone to collapsing. Older, established leaves usually look completely normal.

## Where on the plant symptoms appear

The newest growth only, because calcium is immobile and cannot be relocated from
older tissue once it is built into a leaf. Mature leaves lower on the plant are
unaffected even when new growth is clearly abnormal.

## Look-alikes and how to tell them apart

Iron deficiency also targets new growth but produces interveinal yellowing with
distinct green veins, not physical distortion or dead patches; the new leaves stay
a normal shape. Underwatering can curl leaves too, but it affects the whole plant
at once and the curl relaxes within hours of watering, unlike the permanent
crinkling calcium shortage causes only in new growth. Low humidity crisps leaf
edges on leaves of any age rather than selectively deforming only the newest ones.

## Confirming test the user can perform

Look closely at the one or two newest leaves. If they are puckered, crinkled, or
have small dead patches near the tip while mature leaves further down the stem are
flat and normal in shape, calcium deficiency is likely.

## Treatment, least-invasive first

Check watering consistency first, since irregular watering interferes with
calcium uptake even when calcium is present in the soil; aim for steady, even
moisture rather than alternating drought and soaking. Refresh old potting mix,
which can become depleted of calcium over time. If distortion continues in new
growth, apply a fertiliser that includes calcium, or add crushed eggshell or
garden lime to the soil, following the product label's rate exactly.

## Expected time to visible improvement

New leaves should emerge normally shaped within four to six weeks of steadying
watering and feeding.

## Prognosis and when to give up

Good once watering is made consistent, which is the most common underlying cause.
Persistent distortion despite steady watering points to a genuine soil shortage.
