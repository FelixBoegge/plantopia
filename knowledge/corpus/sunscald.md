## Symptoms

Pale, bleached, or crisp brown patches appear on leaves, with sharply defined
edges marking where light-scorched tissue meets healthy tissue. The patches are
papery and dry rather than soft or water-soaked. Symptoms follow soon after a
move to brighter light, a sunny window, or time outdoors.

## Where on the plant symptoms appear

The side of the leaf and the side of the plant facing the strongest light. Leaves
that were shaded, or new growth that emerged after the move, are typically
unaffected.

## Look-alikes and how to tell them apart

Fertiliser burn browns tips and margins across many leaves after feeding, not
sun-facing patches tied to a light change. Heat stress causes general wilting and
crisping from high temperature rather than sharply bordered bleached patches.
Frost damage blackens tissue after cold exposure and looks water-soaked before it
dries, unlike sunscald's dry, papery patches from the start.

## Confirming test the user can perform

Check whether the bleached or brown patches sit only on the side of the leaf
facing the strongest light, and whether the plant was moved to brighter conditions
or outdoors within the last week or two. Both together confirm sunscald.

## Treatment, least-invasive first

Move the plant back to slightly lower light, or add a sheer curtain to diffuse
direct sun. When increasing light in future, do it gradually over one to two
weeks rather than all at once. Leave damaged tissue in place, since it does not
spread and removing leaves reduces the plant's ability to photosynthesise while
it recovers.

## Expected time to visible improvement

Existing scorched patches do not heal, but new growth under corrected light
should emerge undamaged within two to three weeks.

## Prognosis and when to give up

Excellent, since the damage is cosmetic and confined to affected leaves. Only a
concern if scorch covers most of the plant's leaf area at once.
