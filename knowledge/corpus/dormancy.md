## Symptoms

Growth slows or pauses entirely, most often in autumn and winter as days
shorten and temperatures drop. Some species drop a portion of their leaves on a
seasonal cycle. The plant otherwise looks stable: no wilting, no spotting, no
soft patches, and no pest activity.

## Where on the plant symptoms appear

No particular leaf-position pattern; the whole plant simply slows down together,
which is the main clue that distinguishes it from a disorder.

## Look-alikes and how to tell them apart

Insufficient light also slows growth, but it comes with leaning toward a window
and pale, smaller new leaves, not the general, stable pause dormancy causes.
Overwatering during a dormant period is a common trap: the plant needs much less
water while resting, so a previously normal watering routine can produce the wet
soil and yellowing overwatering causes. Nitrogen deficiency progressively yellows
old leaves due to a genuine shortage, unrelated to day length or season.

## Confirming test the user can perform

Check the season and recent day length. If growth has slowed gradually as days
shortened and temperatures dropped, without wilting, spotting, pests, or
soggy soil, and the species is known to rest seasonally, dormancy is the likely
explanation.

## Treatment, least-invasive first

Reduce watering frequency to match the slower uptake, since dormant roots use far
less water than actively growing ones. Hold off on fertilising until active
growth resumes. Keep the plant in its usual light without forcing extra warmth or
light. Resume normal watering and feeding gradually as new growth appears with
the change of season.

## Expected time to visible improvement

Growth resumes on its own as day length and temperature increase, typically over
several weeks at the start of the growing season.

## Prognosis and when to give up

Excellent. This is entirely normal seasonal behaviour and needs no correction
beyond adjusted watering.
