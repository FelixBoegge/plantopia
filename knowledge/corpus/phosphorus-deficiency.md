## Symptoms

Growth is slow and new leaves are noticeably smaller than older ones, with shorter
spacing between leaves along the stem. Older leaves can take on a dull, dark green
or bronze-purple cast, sometimes most visible on the leaf underside or stalk. The
plant overall looks stunted rather than sick.

## Where on the plant symptoms appear

Older, lower leaves show the discoloration first, since phosphorus is mobile and
is redirected to new growth. The effect on growth rate, however, is visible
throughout the plant as smaller, closer-together new leaves.

## Look-alikes and how to tell them apart

Nitrogen deficiency also fades old leaves but produces even pale yellowing, not a
dark bronze-purple tint, and does not shorten leaf spacing the way phosphorus
shortage does. Cold draught can cause a similar purple-red tint but appears
suddenly on the side of the plant nearest the cold source, not gradually across
older leaves. Natural senescence dulls single old leaves individually without
stunting new growth or shortening internodes.

## Confirming test the user can perform

Compare the spacing between the newest leaves on the stem with growth from a few
months ago, if you have a memory or photo of it. Then check the undersides of the
two or three oldest leaves for a dull bronze or purplish cast rather than plain
green.

## Treatment, least-invasive first

Rule out cold temperatures and low light first, since both slow growth and can
produce similar discoloration without a true nutrient shortage. Refresh old
potting mix at the next repot. Add compost or bone meal to build phosphorus
levels gradually. If growth remains stunted, apply a fertiliser with a higher
phosphorus ratio, following the product label's rate and frequency exactly.

## Expected time to visible improvement

New leaves should emerge larger and more normally spaced within three to six
weeks of correcting feeding.

## Prognosis and when to give up

Good, though slower to resolve than nitrogen deficiency because phosphorus moves
less freely through soil. Persisting stunted growth after several weeks of
correct feeding points toward a root-bound or root-damage cause instead.
