## Symptoms

Adult weevils notch leaf edges at night with small, neat, semicircular bites,
leaving a scalloped outline. More seriously, their larvae live in the soil and
feed on roots, which can cause a plant to wilt and collapse suddenly despite
normal watering, since the root system has been eaten away below ground.

## Where on the plant symptoms appear

Leaf-edge notching can appear anywhere on the foliage. Root damage from larvae
is invisible above ground until the plant suddenly wilts, since roots are
consumed from below rather than showing gradual leaf-position symptoms.

## Look-alikes and how to tell them apart

Slugs and snails and caterpillars also chew leaves, but their holes are larger
and irregular or ragged rather than the small, neat semicircular notches vine
weevil adults leave along the edge. Root rot also causes sudden wilting, but the
roots are mushy and dark rather than bitten away, and the soil is typically wet
rather than the moisture being normal.

## Confirming test the user can perform

Check leaf edges for small, evenly spaced semicircular notches. If the plant has
wilted suddenly with no change in watering, tip it out of its pot and inspect
the soil and roots for cream-coloured, C-shaped grubs with brown heads feeding
on the root mass.

## Treatment, least-invasive first

Hand-pick adult weevils found on the plant or nearby surfaces at night with a
torch, since they are active after dark and hide by day. If larvae are found in
the soil, remove the plant, wash off the old soil, cut away any damaged roots,
and repot into fresh medium. Beneficial nematodes applied as a soil drench
target larvae effectively as a biological control. Only if the infestation
persists, apply a soil-applied insecticide labelled for vine weevil, following
the product label exactly.

## Expected time to visible improvement

Leaf notching stops once adults are removed. Root recovery after larval damage
takes several weeks to months depending on how much root mass was lost.

## Prognosis and when to give up

Guarded if larval root damage is extensive and the plant has already collapsed;
good if caught early, either at the adult notching stage or before larvae have
consumed most of the root system.
