## Symptoms

Stems grow abnormally long and thin, with wide spacing between leaves along the
stem. Leaves are smaller and paler than normal. Growth leans strongly toward the
nearest light source and can become weak enough to flop over under its own
weight.

## Where on the plant symptoms appear

Concentrated in recent growth: the newest section of stem is thinnest, palest,
and most widely spaced, while older growth further down retains its original,
tighter leaf spacing.

## Look-alikes and how to tell them apart

Insufficient light covers the broader picture of slowed growth, paling, and
leaning without necessarily producing the dramatic stem stretching etiolation
shows; etiolation is the extreme, most visible endpoint of prolonged low light,
specifically the thin, widely spaced stem morphology itself. Dormancy pauses
growth altogether rather than producing rapid, weak, reaching growth. Transplant
shock wilts and drops leaves without the elongated, widely spaced stem pattern.

## Confirming test the user can perform

Measure the spacing between the most recent few leaves and compare it with older
growth further down the stem. Noticeably longer gaps, a thinner stem, and paler
colour in the new growth, together with obvious leaning toward a light source,
confirms etiolation.

## Treatment, least-invasive first

Move the plant to significantly brighter light immediately, or add a grow light
on a timer. Stake or support weak, stretched stems that are flopping over. Once
light is corrected, prune back the leggiest growth to encourage bushier new
growth from lower, stronger nodes. No biological or chemical treatment applies.

## Expected time to visible improvement

New growth should emerge with normal spacing and colour within four to six weeks
of increasing light. Existing elongated stems do not shorten on their own.

## Prognosis and when to give up

Excellent once light is corrected. Pruning back leggy growth and letting the
plant regrow is usually faster than waiting for existing weak stems to
strengthen.
