## Symptoms

A white or pale crust gradually accumulates on the soil surface, around the pot
rim, or at the drainage holes over weeks to months. Leaf tips and margins slowly
brown and growth loses vigour even though the plant is fed on a normal schedule.
The onset is gradual rather than sudden.

## Where on the plant symptoms appear

General margin and tip browning across the plant, without a strong age pattern,
building up slowly rather than appearing overnight.

## Look-alikes and how to tell them apart

Fertiliser burn produces the same crust and margin browning but appears suddenly
within days of a single feeding; salt buildup accumulates gradually from repeated
feeding or mineral-rich tap water over a much longer period. Potassium deficiency
also browns margins slowly on old leaves, but leaves no crust because it is a
nutrient shortage rather than a mineral excess. Low humidity crisps tips gradually
too, but with no crust anywhere on the soil or pot.

## Confirming test the user can perform

Look at the soil surface and the rim of the pot for a white or pale crust. Dampen
a spot of it with a fingertip: mineral crust stays chalky and does not simply wash
away like ordinary soil residue would.

## Treatment, least-invasive first

Flush the soil thoroughly with plenty of plain water, repeating two or three
times over a day so water runs freely from the drainage holes each time.
Physically scrape off visible crust from the pot rim and soil surface. Switch to
rainwater, distilled water, or filtered water if tap water is very hard. Reduce
fertiliser frequency going forward and flush the soil periodically as routine
maintenance.

## Expected time to visible improvement

Growth should regain vigour within two to four weeks of flushing and switching
water source. Existing browned tips do not recover.

## Prognosis and when to give up

Very good once flushed and the water source is addressed. Recurrence points to
consistently hard tap water or over-frequent feeding rather than a one-off cause.
