## Symptoms

Leaves turn a uniform pale green or yellow, veins included, rather than showing a
blotchy or streaked pattern. Growth slows and new leaves emerge smaller than usual.
In advanced cases the oldest leaves yellow completely and drop.

## Where on the plant symptoms appear

The oldest, lowest leaves first, because nitrogen is mobile and the plant pulls it
from old growth to feed new growth. The newest leaves at the growing tip stay green
the longest.

## Look-alikes and how to tell them apart

Overwatering yellows old leaves too, but the soil stays wet and soft brown patches
appear; nitrogen deficiency yellows evenly with normal soil moisture. Magnesium
deficiency also targets old leaves but leaves a blotchy pattern with the veins
staying green; nitrogen yellows the whole leaf, veins included. Natural senescence
yellows one old leaf at a time in isolation, not a broad band of lower leaves
together.

## Confirming test the user can perform

Look at the two or three oldest leaves near the base. If the entire leaf blade,
including the veins, has faded to a uniform pale green or yellow while the newest
leaves stay dark green, and the soil moisture feels normal, nitrogen deficiency is
likely.

## Treatment, least-invasive first

Check that watering and light are adequate first, since poor uptake often mimics
true shortage. Refresh old, depleted potting mix at the next repot. Work in
compost or worm castings to add nitrogen gradually and improve the medium overall.
If growth does not respond, apply a balanced liquid fertiliser formulated for
foliage growth, following the product label's rate and frequency exactly.

## Expected time to visible improvement

New leaves should emerge a healthier green within two to four weeks of correcting
feeding. Existing yellow leaves will not turn green again.

## Prognosis and when to give up

Excellent. Nitrogen deficiency responds reliably and quickly once feeding resumes;
persistent yellowing despite feeding suggests a root problem instead.
