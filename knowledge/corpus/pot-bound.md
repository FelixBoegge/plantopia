## Symptoms

Growth slows or stalls despite good light and feeding. The plant dries out much
faster than it used to, sometimes needing water every day or two. Roots appear at
the drainage holes, circle visibly at the soil surface, or the whole root mass
lifts out of the pot as a dense tangle.

## Where on the plant symptoms appear

No leaf-position pattern — the tell is below the soil line and in watering
behaviour, not in a particular leaf zone. Older plants that have not been repotted
in years are the most common candidates.

## Look-alikes and how to tell them apart

Underwatering also causes fast drying and wilting, but a pot-bound plant dries out
fast because roots have displaced the soil, not because too little water was given;
increasing water volume only delays the same problem. Nutrient deficiency slows
growth too, but feeding a pot-bound plant more does little because there is barely
any soil left to hold the fertiliser.

## Confirming test the user can perform

Tip the plant out of its pot. If you see a dense mass of roots circling the inside
of the pot with little visible soil, or roots that have grown out through the
drainage holes, the plant is pot-bound.

## Treatment, least-invasive first

Move the plant into a pot one or two sizes larger with fresh medium. Gently loosen
the outer layer of circling roots by hand before repotting so they grow outward
instead of continuing to spiral. Trim only the thickest, most tangled outer roots
if the mass is extremely dense. Water thoroughly after repotting and resume normal
feeding once new growth appears.

## Expected time to visible improvement

Watering frequency should normalise within one to two weeks. Renewed vigorous
growth typically appears within four to six weeks as roots establish into the new
medium.

## Prognosis and when to give up

Excellent. This is one of the most reliably fixable disorders — repotting almost
always restores normal growth.
