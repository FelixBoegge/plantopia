## Symptoms

Small, flat, shell-like brown or tan bumps stick motionless to stems and leaf
undersides. A sticky honeydew coats leaves beneath an infestation, sometimes with
black sooty mould growing on it. Leaves yellow and growth slows as colonies grow.

## Where on the plant symptoms appear

Stems and the undersides of leaves along veins, where scale attaches and does
not move once settled. Older stems often carry the heaviest colonies.

## Look-alikes and how to tell them apart

Mealybugs produce similar honeydew but appear as soft, white, cottony masses
rather than hard, flat, immobile bumps. Aphids cluster as visible moving insects,
not fixed shell-like scales. A natural leaf bump, thorn, or growth node stays
firmly attached and cannot be scraped off with a fingernail the way a scale
insect's shell can.

## Confirming test the user can perform

Try to scrape one of the small bumps off a stem with a fingernail. A scale insect
detaches as a flat, shell-like disc, sometimes leaving a sticky mark, whereas a
natural growth feature will not come off.

## Treatment, least-invasive first

Isolate the plant. Scrape or pick off visible scales by hand or with a soft
toothbrush, since their protective shell makes rinsing alone less effective.
Wipe stems and leaf undersides with a cloth dampened in mild soapy water
afterward. Repeat weekly for several weeks to catch newly hatched crawlers, which
lack the protective shell and are easier to remove. Only if the infestation
persists, apply horticultural oil, following the product label exactly.

## Expected time to visible improvement

No new scales within three to four weeks. Honeydew residue can be cleaned once
the colony is under control.

## Prognosis and when to give up

Good but slow, since the shell protects mature scale from most treatments;
consistency over several weeks matters more than the strength of any single
treatment.
