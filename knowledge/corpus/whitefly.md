## Symptoms

Tiny white, moth-like insects rise in a cloud from the leaves when the plant is
disturbed, then resettle on leaf undersides within a minute or two. Leaves
yellow and a sticky honeydew coats foliage below an infestation, sometimes with
sooty mould developing on the residue.

## Where on the plant symptoms appear

Leaf undersides throughout the plant, with the heaviest concentrations on
mid-aged leaves. Honeydew drips onto whatever leaves sit below an infested area.

## Look-alikes and how to tell them apart

Fungus gnats also fly up when disturbed, but they rise from the soil surface,
not the leaves, and are dark rather than white. Aphids cluster on new growth and
leave honeydew too, but they are visible soft-bodied insects that stay put on the
plant rather than a cloud of flying insects that scatters and resettles. Mealybugs
leave similar honeydew but appear as fixed cottony masses rather than flying
insects.

## Confirming test the user can perform

Gently shake the plant or tap a leaf and watch for a cloud of tiny white insects
lifting off and flying briefly before resettling on the leaf undersides within a
minute or two.

## Treatment, least-invasive first

Isolate the plant, since whitefly spreads readily by flying to nearby plants.
Rinse the undersides of leaves thoroughly with water, repeating every few days.
Yellow sticky traps placed near the plant catch flying adults and reduce
numbers. Introduce parasitic wasps bred for whitefly control if the infestation
is heavy. Only as a last resort, apply an insecticidal soap or horticultural oil,
following the product label exactly and treating leaf undersides thoroughly.

## Expected time to visible improvement

Fewer flying adults within one to two weeks of consistent treatment. Honeydew
residue can be wiped away once numbers drop.

## Prognosis and when to give up

Good with persistence. Whitefly populations rebound quickly if treatment stops
before eggs and nymphs on leaf undersides are also cleared.
