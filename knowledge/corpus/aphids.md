## Symptoms

Clusters of small, soft-bodied insects, often green but sometimes black, pink,
or grey, gather on new growth and leaf undersides. Leaves may curl or become
distorted where aphids feed, and a sticky honeydew coats leaves below the
colony. Fine white flecks of shed skin can be seen among the cluster.

## Where on the plant symptoms appear

Concentrated on the newest, softest growth: stem tips, flower buds, and the
undersides of young leaves. Curling and distortion follow the same tender growth.

## Look-alikes and how to tell them apart

Mealybugs also cluster and produce honeydew but appear as white, cottony masses
rather than visible soft-bodied insects in a range of colours. Scale insects sit
motionless as flat, shell-like bumps rather than a moving cluster. Spider mites
cause stippling and webbing without the sticky honeydew or visible clustered
insects aphids leave on new growth.

## Confirming test the user can perform

Look closely at stem tips and the undersides of the newest leaves for a cluster
of small, soft-bodied insects. Touch a sticky leaf below the cluster: honeydew
feels tacky between the fingers, confirming an aphid colony rather than dust or
dirt.

## Treatment, least-invasive first

Isolate the plant. Rinse off visible clusters with a strong jet of water,
focusing on stem tips and leaf undersides, repeating every few days. Wipe
remaining aphids off by hand or with a damp cloth. Introduce ladybirds or
lacewing larvae, which feed readily on aphids, if the infestation is large.
Only if numbers persist, apply an insecticidal soap or horticultural oil,
following the product label exactly.

## Expected time to visible improvement

No new clusters within one to two weeks. Curled or distorted leaves do not
uncurl, but new growth should emerge clean.

## Prognosis and when to give up

Excellent. Aphids are among the easiest common pests to control once isolation
and rinsing begin promptly.
