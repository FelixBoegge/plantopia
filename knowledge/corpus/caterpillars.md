## Symptoms

Irregular holes with ragged, chewed edges appear in leaves, sometimes large
enough to remove most of a leaf overnight. Small dark, roughly round droppings
(frass) collect on leaves below the damage or on the soil surface. A caterpillar
itself can sometimes be found resting on the underside of a damaged leaf.

## Where on the plant symptoms appear

Wherever a caterpillar has been feeding, often concentrated on a few leaves
rather than spread evenly, since a single caterpillar can consume a lot from one
area before moving on.

## Look-alikes and how to tell them apart

Slugs and snails leave holes with smoother edges and a silvery slime trail
rather than the ragged bite marks and dark frass pellets caterpillars leave.
Vine weevil adults notch leaf edges with small, neat semicircular bites rather
than eating irregular holes through the leaf interior. Physical damage from
handling or a fall creates tears or crush marks without frass or a feeding
pattern.

## Confirming test the user can perform

Check the underside of a damaged leaf and the soil or leaves just below it for
small, dark, roughly round droppings. Look closely along the leaf edges and
undersides of nearby leaves for the caterpillar itself, which is often
well camouflaged.

## Treatment, least-invasive first

Inspect the plant thoroughly and hand-pick any caterpillars found, moving them
away from garden plants if the plant is kept outdoors. Check the undersides of
leaves for egg clusters and remove them. Netting over vulnerable outdoor plants
prevents new egg-laying. Only if damage continues and the pest cannot be found
by hand, apply a biological control containing Bacillus thuringiensis, following
the product label exactly.

## Expected time to visible improvement

Damage stops as soon as the caterpillar is removed. Existing holes do not heal,
but new leaves should emerge undamaged.

## Prognosis and when to give up

Excellent once the caterpillar is found and removed, since a single pest is
usually responsible for damage that looks alarming.
