## Symptoms

Leaf tips and margins turn brown and crisp, sometimes curling inward. New leaves
can emerge small or fail to unfurl fully. Symptoms are worse on plants placed near
heating vents, radiators, or air conditioning, and worse in winter when indoor air
is driest.

## Where on the plant symptoms appear

Tips and margins across the whole plant, generally worse on leaves closest to a
heat vent, radiator, or draughty window.

## Look-alikes and how to tell them apart

Potassium deficiency also browns margins, but slowly over weeks on the oldest
leaves specifically, unrelated to nearby heating or air movement. Fertiliser burn
browns margins suddenly after feeding, often with a salt crust on the soil, rather
than gradually from dry air. Spider mites also thrive in dry air and can appear
alongside low humidity, but they cause stippling and webbing rather than simple
crisping.

## Confirming test the user can perform

Check whether the plant sits near a heating vent, radiator, or air conditioner,
and whether the air nearby feels noticeably dry. Look at leaves closest to that
source: if they are more crisped than leaves further away, and there is no salt
crust on the soil, low humidity is likely.

## Treatment, least-invasive first

Move the plant away from heating vents, radiators, and air conditioning outlets.
Group it with other plants or place it on a tray of moist pebbles to raise local
humidity. A room humidifier gives the most reliable and lasting improvement for
plants that need consistently higher humidity than a home naturally provides.

## Expected time to visible improvement

New growth should emerge without tip browning within two to three weeks of
raising humidity. Existing crisped tips do not recover.

## Prognosis and when to give up

Very good once humidity is raised and the plant is moved away from the dry air
source. Persistent browning despite higher humidity suggests a watering or
feeding cause instead.
