## Symptoms

Leaves and stems turn translucent and water-soaked looking, then blacken and
collapse within a day or two of exposure to freezing or near-freezing
temperatures. Affected tissue feels mushy and limp once it has thawed. Whole
sections of the plant can be affected if it was left fully exposed to the cold.

## Where on the plant symptoms appear

Whichever parts were actually exposed: the whole plant if left outside overnight
in cold weather, or only the leaves touching a cold window pane or sitting in an
unheated room.

## Look-alikes and how to tell them apart

Cold draught produces similar blackening but comes from a chilly air current
above freezing, usually confined to one side, and does not start with the
water-soaked translucency frost causes before tissue collapses. Root rot also
produces mushy tissue, but at the stem base and roots, tied to overwatering
rather than a cold event, and the leaves above stay firm at first.

## Confirming test the user can perform

Consider whether the plant was exposed to temperatures at or near freezing in
the last one to two days — left outside overnight, forgotten near a cold
windowpane, or kept in an unheated space. Water-soaked, translucent patches that
then blacken and turn mushy confirm frost.

## Treatment, least-invasive first

Move the plant to a stable, frost-free location immediately. Do not remove
damaged tissue right away; wait a few days to see how much of the plant recovers,
since some tissue that looks bad initially can rebound. Once it is clear which
parts are dead, trim them away with clean scissors. Resume normal watering only
once new, healthy growth appears.

## Expected time to visible improvement

Surviving tissue should show clear signs of recovery within one to two weeks.
Blackened, collapsed tissue will not recover and should eventually be removed.

## Prognosis and when to give up

Depends on exposure severity. If only leaf tips were touched, recovery is
excellent. If stems collapsed and turned black at the base, the plant is unlikely
to survive; take a cutting from any unaffected growth if possible.
