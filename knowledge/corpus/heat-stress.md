## Symptoms

The plant wilts despite moist soil, and leaf edges curl or crisp, sometimes with
scorched-looking patches. Symptoms appear during unusually hot weather or when
the plant sits near a heater, radiator, or appliance that gives off heat. Growth
can pause entirely until temperatures drop.

## Where on the plant symptoms appear

General wilting across the plant, usually worse on the side closest to a heat
source such as a radiator, heater, or hot window.

## Look-alikes and how to tell them apart

Underwatering wilts in a similar way, but the soil is dry; heat stress wilts even
with moist soil, purely from high ambient temperature. Sunscald produces sharply
bordered bleached patches tied to light intensity rather than general wilting from
heat. Low humidity crisps tips gradually over time rather than causing the acute,
whole-plant wilting that a heat spike produces.

## Confirming test the user can perform

Check the room temperature near the plant, especially close to windows,
radiators, or appliances. If the air feels distinctly hot and the soil is still
moist a day after watering, yet the plant is wilting, heat stress rather than
underwatering is the likely cause.

## Treatment, least-invasive first

Move the plant away from the heat source or to a cooler part of the room.
Increase air circulation with a fan set to circulate rather than blow directly on
the plant. Raise humidity slightly, since hot air is often also dry air. Water as
normal once the soil dries at the surface, but do not overcompensate with extra
water while the soil is already moist.

## Expected time to visible improvement

Wilting should ease within a day or two of cooling the environment. Scorched
tissue does not recover, but new growth should be unaffected.

## Prognosis and when to give up

Very good once moved away from the heat source. Repeated heat spikes without
intervention can eventually stress the plant enough to invite pests such as
spider mites, which favour hot, dry conditions.
