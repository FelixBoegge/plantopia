## Symptoms

Irregular holes with smooth, rounded edges appear in leaves, often starting at
the margin. A silvery, dried slime trail is visible on leaves, stems, or nearby
surfaces, especially noticeable in the morning. Damage is usually worse on lower
leaves close to the soil or pot rim.

## Where on the plant symptoms appear

Lower leaves and leaves close to the soil surface, since slugs and snails travel
from the ground up and feed mostly at night close to where they hide by day.

## Look-alikes and how to tell them apart

Caterpillars leave ragged, chewed holes with dark frass pellets rather than
smooth-edged holes and slime trails. Vine weevil adults notch leaf edges with
small, neat semicircular bites rather than larger irregular holes. Physical
damage from handling creates tears without the smooth grazed edge or slime trail
that feeding leaves.

## Confirming test the user can perform

Look for a silvery, dried slime trail on the leaf surface or nearby pot and
surfaces, most visible in the morning light. Check under the pot, saucer, or
nearby mulch and stones with a torch after dark for the slugs or snails
themselves.

## Treatment, least-invasive first

Move the plant away from ground contact if possible, and keep the area around
it clear of damp hiding spots such as mulch or leaf litter. Hand-pick slugs and
snails found at night with a torch and relocate them well away from the plant.
Copper tape around the pot rim deters them mechanically. Beneficial nematodes
that target slugs can be applied to the soil as a biological control. Only if
the problem is severe and outdoors, use a pet-safe slug bait, following the
product label exactly.

## Expected time to visible improvement

New feeding damage should stop within a few days of removing the pests and
their hiding spots.

## Prognosis and when to give up

Excellent. Slug and snail damage is cosmetic and rarely threatens an established
plant's survival once the pests are controlled.
