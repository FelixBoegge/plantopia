## Symptoms

Leaf tips and margins turn brown and crisp suddenly, appearing within days of
feeding, and can affect leaves of every age at once rather than just the oldest.
A white or crusty residue may be visible on the soil surface or pot rim. In severe
cases leaves droop or the whole plant looks scorched.

## Where on the plant symptoms appear

Scattered across the whole plant rather than following an age-based pattern,
because the cause is an external salt excess in the root zone, not a shortage
tied to nutrient mobility.

## Look-alikes and how to tell them apart

Potassium deficiency also browns leaf margins but develops slowly over weeks on
the oldest leaves only, unconnected to a recent feeding and without a salt crust.
Salt buildup produces the same crust and margin browning but accumulates gradually
over months from repeated feeding or hard water, rather than appearing suddenly
after one application. Low humidity crisps tips gradually across the plant with
no link to feeding and no crust.

## Confirming test the user can perform

Check whether the plant was fertilised in the last one to two weeks and look for
a white crust on the soil surface or pot rim. If both are present and the burn
appeared suddenly on leaves of mixed ages, fertiliser burn is the likely cause.

## Treatment, least-invasive first

Stop fertilising immediately. Flush the soil thoroughly with plenty of plain
water, letting it run through the drainage holes several times, to leach excess
salts out of the root zone. Trim off leaves that are more than half brown.
Withhold feeding for several weeks, then resume at a reduced rate and frequency
compared with the product label, increasing gradually only if the plant tolerates
it well.

## Expected time to visible improvement

New growth should emerge undamaged within two to three weeks of flushing.
Existing browned tissue does not recover.

## Prognosis and when to give up

Good if flushed promptly, since the damage is usually confined to leaf tissue.
If roots were exposed to a strong concentration, wilting despite flushing
suggests root damage and a slower recovery, similar to root rot.
