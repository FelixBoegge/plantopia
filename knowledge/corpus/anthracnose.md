## Symptoms

Dark, sunken lesions develop, usually starting at leaf tips, margins, or along
veins, and enlarge with a slightly darker, defined border. In humid conditions
tiny dark fruiting structures can appear in concentric rings within the lesion.
Stems can develop sunken cankers, and shoot tips may die back.

## Where on the plant symptoms appear

Leaf tips, margins, and veins first; in more advanced cases, stems and shoot
tips show dieback where lesions have girdled the tissue.

## Look-alikes and how to tell them apart

Fungal leaf spot produces smaller, rounder spots scattered across the leaf
rather than larger sunken lesions concentrated at tips and margins. Bacterial
leaf spot looks water-soaked and greasy rather than dry and sunken with a
defined border. Frost damage also blackens tips, but starts translucent and
water-soaked before collapsing, without the dry, sunken texture anthracnose
lesions develop over days.

## Confirming test the user can perform

Press gently on a dark lesion at a leaf tip or margin: anthracnose lesions feel
slightly sunken and dry rather than soft or water-soaked. In humid weather, look
for tiny dark dots arranged in rings within the lesion.

## Treatment, least-invasive first

Improve airflow and avoid wetting leaves when watering. Remove and discard
affected leaves and any dead shoot tips, cutting back into clean tissue with
disinfected tools. Clear fallen leaf debris nearby, since it can harbour spores.
Space the plant away from others while active. Only if lesions keep spreading,
apply a fungicide labelled for anthracnose, following the product label exactly.

## Expected time to visible improvement

No new lesions within one to two weeks of improving airflow and removing
affected tissue.

## Prognosis and when to give up

Good if caught before it reaches main stems. Dieback that has progressed into a
main stem or growing point is a poor sign for that section.
