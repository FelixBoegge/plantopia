## Symptoms

Seedlings or very young plants suddenly collapse and topple over. The stem at
the soil line becomes thin, water-soaked, and darkened, as though pinched, just
before or as the seedling falls. Germination itself can also fail, with seeds
rotting before they emerge.

## Where on the plant symptoms appear

Right at the soil line, on the stem base of seedlings or very young plants,
rather than on leaves or established roots the way most disorders present.

## Look-alikes and how to tell them apart

Root rot causes wilting and mushy roots in older, established plants, not the
sudden stem-base collapse of a young seedling. Transplant shock wilts gradually
over days without the thin, pinched, water-soaked stem base damping-off
produces almost overnight. Underwatering wilts a seedling too, but the stem base
stays firm and the seedling recovers once watered, unlike the collapsed, dark
stem damping-off causes regardless of watering.

## Confirming test the user can perform

Examine the stem of a collapsed seedling right at the soil line. A thin,
water-soaked, darkened, pinched section at that exact point confirms
damping-off rather than simple wilting or drought.

## Treatment, least-invasive first

Remove and discard collapsed seedlings immediately to protect the rest of the
tray. Improve airflow around remaining seedlings and let the surface dry
slightly between waterings, since damping-off thrives in constantly wet,
still conditions. Use a sterile, free-draining seed-starting mix for future
sowings and avoid overcrowding seedlings. Only for valuable remaining seedlings,
a fungicide drench labelled for damping-off can be applied, following the
product label exactly.

## Expected time to visible improvement

Remaining healthy seedlings should show no new collapses within a week of
improving airflow and drainage.

## Prognosis and when to give up

Poor for a seedling that has already collapsed at the stem; it will not recover.
Protecting the remaining seedlings promptly is the realistic goal.
