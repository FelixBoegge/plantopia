## Symptoms

Leaves on one side of the plant suddenly droop, blacken, or develop water-soaked
patches, often overnight, while the rest of the plant looks fine. Leaf drop can
follow within days on the affected side. The damage lines up with a door,
window, or vent that lets in cold air.

## Where on the plant symptoms appear

Confined to the side of the plant nearest the draught source, not spread evenly
across the whole plant.

## Look-alikes and how to tell them apart

Frost damage causes similar blackening but follows an actual freezing or
near-freezing exposure and can affect the whole plant if it was fully exposed,
rather than staying confined to one side facing a merely chilly draught.
Transplant shock wilts the plant generally, without a directional pattern tied to
a specific cold source. Chemical damage also causes sudden, localized harm, but
traces to a spray or fume exposure rather than a cold air current.

## Confirming test the user can perform

Check whether the damaged side of the plant faces a door, window, or vent that
was recently opened, is draughty, or sits close to an air conditioner. Hold a
hand near that spot: if the air feels noticeably colder than the rest of the
room, cold draught is the likely cause.

## Treatment, least-invasive first

Move the plant away from the draught source, or seal or weatherstrip the gap
letting cold air in. Trim off leaves that are fully blackened, since they will
not recover and can invite rot if left in place. Keep the plant somewhere with a
stable temperature going forward.

## Expected time to visible improvement

New growth should be unaffected within one to two weeks once the plant is moved.
Damaged leaves do not recover.

## Prognosis and when to give up

Very good, since damage is usually confined to exposed leaves and the plant
resumes normal growth once relocated away from the draught.
